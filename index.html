about

A mechanical engineer who writes the models

I'm a Computer Science PhD candidate in the Vision & AI Lab (VAIL) at the University of Texas at San Antonio, advised by Dr. Amanda Fernandez. My research is multimodal machine learning for industrial inspection and anomaly detection, with a focus on laser powder bed fusion (LPBF).

I came to ML from the shop floor. My undergraduate degree is in mechanical engineering, and I spent three years training more than 100 students on lathes, mills and laser cutters. At Idaho National Laboratory I designed hardware for reactor experiments, and at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory I built interpretable models for sensor data from nuclear fuel recycling. I treat ML as a tool in service of physical systems, and I care whether a model's answer survives contact with the hardware.

I currently hold a Graduate Research Organization (GRO) appointment at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, working with Luke Scime on fast processing of near- and long-wave infrared video from powder bed fusion builds.

  • Learning from very small, expensive datasets, where every printed coupon counts.
  • Fusing modalities (images, sensor traces, process parameters, text) and testing when fusion actually helps.
  • Algorithms fast enough to keep up with a live build instead of a hard drive full of video.
  • Keeping the physics in view: heat transfer, porosity mechanisms, and what the sensor can really see.

Featured · side project · PyTorch

Rebuilding a temperature field from six sensors, with physics as the teacher

A physics-informed neural network that reconstructs a transient temperature field from sparse point sensors and noisy low-resolution camera frames. The heat equation and convective boundary conditions are part of the loss. I checked every result against finite-difference solvers I wrote myself, first on a 2D plate and then on a 3D finned aluminum heat sink.

FINDING 01

Six sensors are enough when the physics is right

With only six point sensors plus the heat equation, the network reconstructs the whole plate. Ten minutes into heating, its error is within about 0.3% of the field's range. The physics fills in everything the sensors can't see.

FINDING 02

Adding a second modality made it worse

I trained the same network three ways: sensors only, camera only, and both fused. Fusion helped early in the transient (0.39 K vs 0.53 K at 88 s). Later on, the noisy camera pulled the fit toward its own noise, and sensors alone came out ahead (0.25 K vs 0.53 K at 600 s).

A second modality only pays off when it fills a gap the stronger one can't see. That question drives my PhD work too.

FINDING 03

The loss curve said it worked. It hadn't.

On the heat sink, physics-only training converged with clean loss curves, yet the fins never warmed above 0 K. The heat source is a 1.5 mm spot in a 28 mm domain split across 13 fins. Random collocation points almost never landed on it, so a cold field satisfied the equation nearly everywhere the network checked.

Concentrating samples at the source and adding six virtual sensors cut max error from ~11 K to ~4 K on a field that peaks near 13 K. That's still imperfect, and the figure shows where: the outer fins run warm.

When does multimodal fusion help anomaly detection?

A controlled comparison of 8 fusion mechanisms for image-and-text industrial anomaly detection across 5 datasets. As samples get harder, detection holds steady while cross-modal retrieval collapses, on the same images, embeddings and model. Aggregate metrics hide this failure.

Real-time processing of thermal video from powder bed fusion

Near- and long-wave infrared cameras record each layer as it melts and cools; those signatures track flaw populations and microstructure. I develop and accelerate compositing algorithms that reduce ~100 GB of LWIR/NIR video to useful measurements within minutes, so processing keeps pace with acquisition.

UTSA · SPIE 2026

Multimodal porosity prediction in LPBF

Energy density alone doesn't explain porosity. I combined process parameters with build imagery and compared fusion strategies on a small dataset. Image features hurt regression but clearly helped classifying the porosity regime.

DynMM: 96.7% recall · 91.7% accuracy (regime classification)
